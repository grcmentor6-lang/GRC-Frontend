1\. Purpose and authority

This Charter establishes the Governance, Risk and Compliance (GRC) function of BKB Holding Company ("BKB" or "the Group"). It defines the mandate, scope, authority, structure, roles, operating model, reporting lines and performance expectations of the Group GRC function and of the GRC functions within each of the Group's portfolio organisations.

The Charter is issued under the authority of the BKB Board of Directors. It is binding on every organisation in which BKB holds a controlling interest and on every office of those organisations. Where local law imposes a stricter requirement than this Charter, local law prevails and the stricter requirement is recorded in the organisation addendum (see the Group Policy Library, BKB-POL-000).

Mission. The Group GRC function exists to (1) protect the confidentiality, integrity and availability of information and services across the portfolio, (2) keep every organisation lawful in every jurisdiction in which it operates, (3) make risk visible and decisions defensible at Board level, and (4) serve as the real-world practice environment for the BKB GRC Mentorship Programmes (GRC 101, 301 and 501).

2\. Scope

This Charter applies to all 42 organisations in the BKB portfolio, operating across 12 industries, 168 offices (42 head offices and 126 regional offices) in 51 countries on six continents. It covers all people (employees, contractors, AI agents acting on behalf of the Group), processes, information assets (7,663 registered assets), technology (IT, OT, cloud, AI) and third parties that create, process, store or transmit Group information.

4\. Guiding principles

Risk-based and proportionate — the depth of control follows criticality (HIGH \> MEDIUM \> LOW) and asset criticality (Mission Critical \> Business Critical \> Standard \> Non-Critical).

Comply once, satisfy many — one Common Control Framework (CCF) anchored on ISO/IEC 27001:2022 Annex A, mapped to every mandatory and optional standard, so evidence is collected once and reused.

Local law first — the strictest applicable requirement (Group, national or sectoral) wins.

Three lines of accountability — management owns risk; GRC oversees and challenges; internal audit provides independent assurance.

Human accountability for AI — AI agents perform GRC work, but a named human is accountable for every decision with legal, financial or safety effect.

Evidence over assertion — a control is only "effective" when tested evidence exists.

Privacy by design and by default across all processing of personal data.

Transparency — risk, incidents and compliance status are reported honestly and promptly, without fear of blame.

Learning organisation — every activity is designed so that it can be used as a structured mentorship exercise.

5\. GRC operating model

5.1 Federated hub-and-spoke model

BKB operates a federated GRC model. The Group GRC Office (hub) sets direction, standards and methods, and provides shared services. Each portfolio organisation (spoke) owns and operates its GRC programme through its Governance, Risk & Compliance department (owner: Chief Risk & Compliance Officer) and Information Security (CISO Office). Each regional office operates a Regional Compliance & Legal function and a Regional IT & Security Operations function that execute locally and report to the organisation head office.

| Layer | Who | Accountabilities |
| :-: | :-: | :-: |
| Group (hub) | Group GRC Office — AI-agent GRC department of 15 NICE-aligned roles, supervised by human Group executives | Charter, policy library, Common Control Framework, ERM framework, risk appetite, group reporting, shared tooling, mentorship programmes, oversight of all 42 orgs |
| Organisation (spoke) | Org CEO; Chief Risk & Compliance Officer; CISO; DPO; Head of Internal Audit | Implement Group framework; maintain ISMS/PIMS and certifications; own org risk register; regulator liaison in HQ country |
| Regional office | Regional Managing Director; Regional Compliance Officer; Regional IT Manager | Local legal compliance; local risk register; incident first response; local regulator and law-enforcement liaison |
| Department | Department owners (as recorded in the Information Asset Register) | Asset ownership, classification, access approval, first-line control operation |

8\. Governance of AI agents

Because GRC work is executed by AI agents, the Group applies the following controls, aligned to ISO/IEC 42001:2023 (AI management system) and the NIST AI Risk Management Framework:

Human-in-the-loop: decisions with legal, regulatory, financial (\> USD 50k), safety or employment effect require approval by a named human accountable owner.

Scope of authority: each agent has a written mandate, least-privilege access and read-only access to production by default.

Traceability: all agent actions, prompts, sources and outputs are logged immutably for 7 years (or longer where law requires).

Quality assurance: a 10% random sample of agent outputs (100% for HIGH-criticality orgs) is reviewed by a qualified human professional monthly.

Model risk management: agents are inventoried, risk-rated, validated before release and re-validated after material change.

Transparency: stakeholders are informed when they interact with an AI agent; regulators are never corresponded with by an agent without human sign-off.

Kill-switch: the Group CISO may suspend any agent immediately.

10.1 Rules of engagement for mentees

Mentees work only on simulated or sanitised copies of organisation artefacts; no live personal data, credentials, or production access.

Every mentee task is drawn from the task libraries and tagged with level (101/301/501), NICE role, persona and method.

Mentee outputs never become official records without review and approval by the accountable role owner.

Mentees sign a confidentiality and acceptable-use undertaking before onboarding.

Assessment: 50% AI-agent assessment against rubric, 50% human professional assessment; results feed the knowledge-retention metrics (Method Q.vii).

12\. Risk management

Enterprise risk is managed under the BKB Enterprise Risk Management Framework (BKB-ERM-001), aligned to ISO 31000:2018 and COSO ERM 2017, with information-security risk assessed under ISO/IEC 27005:2022. Risk assessments are performed for every office (168 in total) and consolidated to organisation, industry cluster and Group level.

| Criticality band | Risk-assessment frequency | Board reporting | Assurance depth |
| :-: | :-: | :-: | :-: |
| HIGH | Quarterly refresh; continuous KRIs | Quarterly — named risks | Annual external + internal audit; red team annually |
| MEDIUM | Semi-annual refresh | Quarterly — aggregated | Annual internal audit; external certification |
| LOW | Annual refresh | Semi-annual — aggregated | Biennial internal audit; external certification |

13\. Compliance, assurance and audit

Compliance Managers maintain the control library and run the annual compliance testing plan.

Information Security Auditors perform risk-based internal audits; Internal Audit has unrestricted access to records, systems, people and premises.

External certifications and attestations (ISO/IEC 27001, SOC 2 Type II, BSI C5, ISMS-P, PCI DSS, etc.) are scheduled on a Group calendar to avoid lapse.

Findings are rated (Critical/High/Medium/Low) with remediation deadlines of 30/60/90/180 days respectively and tracked to closure with validation.

14\. Incident, crisis and regulatory notification

Incidents are managed under the Incident Management Policy and playbooks. Because the portfolio spans 51 countries, statutory notification clocks start at detection and the shortest applicable clock governs (examples: 1 hour for major incidents in China; 6 hours to CERT-In in India; 12 hours under Hong Kong's critical-infrastructure ordinance; 24 hours early warning under NIS2; 72 hours under GDPR; 4 business days for SEC-registered issuers). The per-country notification matrix is maintained in the ERM framework and in each organisation addendum.

The Group GRC function and org GRC functions have unrestricted access to all records, systems, premises and personnel needed to fulfil this Charter, subject to legal privilege, classification rules and data-protection law.

The Group CISO may escalate any unresolved risk directly to the Board.

Appendix D — Source data observations

The following inconsistencies were found in the source workbooks while preparing this Charter. They do not change the Charter but should be corrected in the master data:

Departments\_by\_Region.xlsx is titled "AURIANA Portfolio"; the context document names the holding company "BKB". This Charter uses BKB.

United Telecommunications Systems: the Nairobi (Kenya) regional office is tagged "Asia-Pacific" in Organization\_Profiles.xlsx; it has been treated as Africa.

The "Key Standards" column in Organization\_Criticality\_Segregation.xlsx differs from the Standards sheet of Organization\_Profiles.xlsx for several organisations (e.g., CloudTech: NIS2 is mandatory in Profiles but not listed in Criticality). The Profiles Standards sheet is treated as authoritative for mandatory/optional status.

Industry naming varies ("Legal, Accounting, & Consulting Services" vs "Legal, Accounting & Consulting Services"; "BPO & KPO"). Normalised in reporting.

Some standards appear under several spellings (e.g., "GDPR (EU)", "GDPR (EU) 2016/679", "GDPR (for EU client data)"; "CobIT 2019" / "COBIT 2019"). The Obligations Register normalises these to a single canonical entry with scope notes.

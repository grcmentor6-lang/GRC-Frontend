Contents

  

1\. Purpose and alignment

This framework defines how BKB identifies, assesses, treats, monitors and reports risk across its 42 organisations, 168 offices and 51 countries. It implements the Enterprise Risk Management Policy (BKB-POL-002) and is aligned to ISO 31000:2018 (principles and process), COSO ERM 2017 (strategy and performance integration), ISO/IEC 27005:2022 (information-security risk), NIST SP 800-30 Rev.1 (risk-assessment conduct) and FAIR (quantification).

2\. Principles

Risk management is integrated into strategy, planning, projects and operations.

Risk is owned by management (first line) and overseen by GRC (second line).

Assessments are evidence-based, repeatable and comparable across the portfolio.

Appetite is explicit, approved by the Board and differentiated by criticality band.

Information on risk is timely, transparent and escalated without delay.

The framework is continually improved toward quantitative management (maturity Level 4).

6\. Assessment methodology

6.1 Process

Establish context (office, assets, processes, obligations, country profile).

Identify risks (scenario library + asset-based + workshop).

Analyse likelihood and impact (inherent).

Assess existing controls and residual risk.

Evaluate against appetite.

Treat (mitigate, transfer, avoid, accept) with owner and due date.

Monitor through KRIs; review per band frequency.

Communicate and report.

6.5 Office risk-assessment model used for the 168 baseline assessments

Inputs — each office's registered assets (count, Mission Critical, Tier 4, personal data, SaaS/cloud/on-prem hosting) from the Information Asset Register; departments; country profile scores (regulatory intensity, geopolitical, natural hazard, infrastructure, cyber threat, legal/enforceability — Annex B); industry threat profile; organisation criticality band; mandatory standards.

Scenario library — 30 scenarios with explicit applicability rules (e.g., OT scenario only for manufacturing, energy, transport and telecom; AML only where relevant; classified-hosting scenario where government markings are hosted on commercial platforms; mentee-programme scenario for LOW orgs). Each office receives 19–27 scenarios.

Scoring — base likelihood and impact per scenario adjusted by country scores, asset profile and band, bounded to 1–5.

Residual — Group controls at maturity Level 3 reduce likelihood by one step; impact-reducing controls also reduce impact by one step.

Evaluation — residual compared with band appetite; above-appetite risks require treatment with due dates of 30/90/180 days for Critical/High/Medium.

Validation — the model provides a consistent baseline; risk owners validate ratings in the first review cycle.

6.6 Risk quantification

HIGH and MEDIUM organisations quantify their top risks using FAIR: Loss Event Frequency (threat event frequency × vulnerability) and Loss Magnitude (primary and secondary loss), simulated with Monte Carlo to produce annualised loss exposure (P50/P90). Results inform investment prioritisation (ROSI) and cyber-insurance limits.

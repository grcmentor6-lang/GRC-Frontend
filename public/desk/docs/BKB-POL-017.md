1\. Purpose

Ensures that every BKB organisation processes personal data lawfully, fairly and transparently, records each processing activity, assesses high-risk processing before it starts, answers data-subject requests on time and moves personal data across borders only under an approved mechanism.

2\. Scope and applicability

All personal data that a Group organisation processes, as controller or as processor, in any system, office or supplier, and every person who handles it.

This policy applies to every BKB organisation. Organisation addenda (BKB-ADD-01 … 42) record local legal requirements that are stricter than, or additional to, this policy. Where law and this policy conflict, the stricter requirement applies.

3\. Policy statements

3.1 Principles

Personal data is processed in line with the principles of GDPR Art.5: lawfulness, fairness and transparency; purpose limitation; data minimisation; accuracy; storage limitation; integrity and confidentiality; and accountability.

Every processing activity has a documented lawful basis for each of its purposes, recorded in its entry of the record of processing activities (RoPA). For an activity the organisation carries out as processor, the entry records the lawful basis the controller relies on for each purpose; the controller confirms it in the data processing agreement. The lawful basis is chosen by asking the questions below in order: the first yes gives the basis.

| Order | Ask | If yes, the lawful basis is |
| :-: | :-: | :-: |
| 1 | Does a law the controller is subject to require it to do this? | Legal obligation (GDPR Art.6(1)(c)) |
| 2 | Is it necessary to perform a contract the person is party to? For an employer's staff, this is their employment contract: what their job requires them to do. | Contract (GDPR Art.6(1)(b)) |
| 3 | Is it optional, done only because the person chose it, and can they withdraw at any time with nothing happening to them? | Consent (GDPR Art.6(1)(a)) |
| 4 | Otherwise: is it in the controller's legitimate interest, in a way the person would reasonably expect, and does that interest not override their rights? Record the balancing test. | Legitimate interests (GDPR Art.6(1)(f)) |

Each organisation keeps a RoPA entry for every processing activity (GDPR Art.30), reviewed whenever the processing changes and at least once a year. Its data-flow map shows where the personal data is collected, the systems that hold it, every transfer and where it is deleted, with the system names of the asset register.

DPIAs are mandatory for high-risk processing, including children's data and AI (procedure Section 6.2, rule R29). Every processing activity is screened before it starts, and the screening is recorded in its RoPA entry, whatever its result.

Data-subject requests are answered within the shortest deadline that applies to the person (procedure Section 6.1): one month under GDPR, 30 days under KVKK, 10 days under PIPA.

Personal data leaves the country of the office that holds it only under the cross-border transfer mechanism that the organisation addendum's Section 2 names for that office: the office the data leaves, not the office that receives it. A transfer is any movement of personal data out of that country, including when people in another country view it or download it remotely.

Children's data is processed only with verifiable parental consent where the law requires it (COPPA 312.5, GDPR Art.8, KVKK).

3.2 Roles

For each processing activity the organisation records which role it holds. The role is decided by who decides why and how the personal data is processed, not by who holds it or who runs the system.

| Role | What it means in this policy |
| :-: | :-: |
| Controller | Decides why the personal data is processed and how (its purposes and means), alone (GDPR Art.4(7)). |
| Joint controller | Decides the purposes and means together with another controller, under an arrangement between them (GDPR Art.26). |
| Processor | Processes personal data on behalf of a controller, only on the controller's documented instructions and for the controller's purposes, under a data processing agreement (GDPR Art.4(8), Art.28). |
| Sub-processor | A processor engaged by a processor, with the controller's authorisation, to carry out part of the processing (GDPR Art.28(2) and (4)). |

An organisation that uses the personal data for a purpose of its own becomes a controller for that purpose, and records it as a separate processing activity.

| Role | Responsibility |

| All workforce | Process personal data only as their role requires, and report a suspected breach at once |

6\. Procedures

6.1 Procedure: Data-subject request handling

| Step | Action | Responsible | Output / SLA |
| :-: | :-: | :-: | :-: |
| 1 | Log the request and verify the person's identity, using only what the organisation already holds | Privacy Compliance Analyst | Request logged — within 1 working day |
| 2 | Find the person's data through the RoPA entries and the systems they name | System owners | Data located — within 10 days |
| 3 | Answer the request: access, correction, erasure, restriction, portability or objection | DPO | Answer sent — within the shortest applicable deadline (Section 3.1) |
| 4 | Where the organisation is processor, pass the request to the controller and help it answer | Privacy Compliance Analyst | Request passed on — within 2 working days |

6.2 Procedure: DPIA

| Step | Action | Responsible | Output / SLA |
| :-: | :-: | :-: | :-: |
| 1 | Screen the processing with rule R29: for each trigger in the table below, is it involved? Any trigger involved means a DPIA is required. Record the screening in the RoPA entry. | Privacy Compliance Analyst | DPIA screening — before the processing starts |
| 2 | Where a DPIA is required, describe the processing, assess necessity and proportionality, and assess the risks to people | Privacy Compliance Analyst with the business owner | DPIA draft — within 15 working days |
| 3 | Decide the measures that reduce each risk, and the residual risk | DPO and Org CISO | Measures agreed |
| 4 | Where a high residual risk remains, consult the supervisory authority before the processing starts (GDPR Art.36) | DPO | Prior consultation — before start |

Rule R29's triggers are read as this policy defines them:

| Trigger (rule R29) | What it means in this policy |
| :-: | :-: |
| Children or students | Data about children (anyone under 18), or about pupils and students enrolled at a school, college or university, whatever their age. |
| AI, analytics or profiling | Evaluating personal aspects of a person by automated means: predicting their future performance, behaviour, interests or reliability, or ranking or grouping people by them, or a recommendation or decision made by an AI model. A mark or a report that only records what a person did is not. |
| Monitoring or recording | Systematic observation of people: their behaviour, communications, location or screens, for example CCTV, e-mail or keystroke monitoring, webcam proctoring or recorded sessions. |
| Health data | Data about a person's physical or mental health, including disability and sickness absence (GDPR Art.9). |
| Identity checks (KYC/AML) | Checking who a person is against identity documents or official records: passports, ID cards, selfies, or KYC or AML screening. |

7\. Guidelines

7.1 Guideline: Privacy by design checklist

Before a new processing activity starts, or an existing one changes, check that it collects only the data its purposes need, sets a retention period for each category, gives people a notice before collection, restricts access to the roles that need it, encrypts the data at rest and in transit, and has a RoPA entry and a DPIA screening.

8\. Compliance, exceptions and enforcement

Compliance is measured through the KPIs below, control testing, internal audit and the GRC Operational Task Libraries.

Exceptions require a documented risk assessment, compensating controls, a named owner, an expiry date (maximum 12 months) and approval per the delegated authority in the GRC Charter Section 6.1.

Violations may result in disciplinary action in accordance with local employment law, contract termination for third parties, and reporting to authorities where legally required.

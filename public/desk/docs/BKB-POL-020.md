1\. Purpose

Ensures that every person who works for a BKB organisation knows the information security policy, their part in it and how to report a security event, and that the people in key ISMS roles are competent for them, with the evidence kept.

2\. Scope and applicability

All workforce of every BKB organisation: employees, contractors and temporary staff, in every office. It covers onboarding training, yearly awareness campaigns, phishing simulations and the competence of the ISMS roles.

This policy applies to every BKB organisation. Organisation addenda (BKB-ADD-01 … 42) record local legal requirements that are stricter than, or additional to, this policy. Where law and this policy conflict, the stricter requirement applies.

3\. Policy statements

3.1 Policy statements

Every new joiner completes security awareness training within their first 30 days, and all workforce at least once a year (ISO/IEC 27001:2022 clause 7.3, Annex A 6.3).

Each office runs at least one awareness campaign a year, built from the Group's 4-week template and content bank (rule R31). A campaign is scenario-based: it starts from a situation staff meet in their own work.

Phishing simulations are approved before they start (procedure Section 6.1). They use fictional brands only, never a real company's name or logo. Results are reported by team, never by name, and nobody is sanctioned for a click: a click leads to a short lesson.

The people in the ISMS roles of Ref-Competence (risk owners, control owners, the ISMS manager) have the competence their role requires; where they do not, action is taken and its effect checked, and the evidence of competence is kept (ISO/IEC 27001:2022 clause 7.2).

Where NIS2 applies, the members of the management body follow security training themselves (NIS2 Art.20(2)).

6\. Procedures

6.1 Procedure: Phishing simulation cycle

| Step | Action | Responsible | Output / SLA |
| :-: | :-: | :-: | :-: |
| 1 | Choose the pretext from current threats to the office, under a fictional brand, matching the campaign's theme | Cyber Threat Intelligence Analyst with the Security Awareness & Training Specialist | Pretext — 2 weeks before launch |
| 2 | Obtain the approvals rule R31 requires for the office, before anything is sent | Security Awareness & Training Specialist | Approvals on file — before launch |
| 3 | Send the simulation in the campaign's simulation week (rule R31), inside working hours, and measure the click rate and the report rate (Section 7.1) | Security Awareness & Training Specialist | Results — within 5 working days of sending |
| 4 | Give a just-in-time micro-lesson to everyone who clicked, and report the results by team in the campaign's last week | Security Awareness & Training Specialist | Results and refresher — the last week |

7\. Guidelines

7.1 Guideline: Effective awareness

A micro-lesson takes no more than 5 minutes to read (about 400 words). It opens with the theme's scenario card, names the three behaviours staff are asked to adopt, and ends with three check questions, each testing one of the behaviours.

People learn from what they practise and from feedback, not from fear. Messages explain what to do, never who failed.

Learning shows in what staff do. Reporting a suspicious message is the behaviour a campaign sets out to grow, so the report rate is followed from campaign to campaign. A falling click rate on its own can mean only that the lure was easy to spot.

Each campaign is measured against the targets below. A rate counts people, out of all the staff the simulation was sent to.

| Measure | Target | How it is measured |
| :-: | :-: | :-: |
| Click rate | The KRI of the phishing risk in the office risk register | Staff who clicked the simulated message ÷ staff it was sent to × 100 |
| Report rate | ≥ 60 % | Staff who reported the simulated message ÷ staff it was sent to × 100 |

8\. Compliance, exceptions and enforcement

Compliance is measured through the KPIs below, control testing, internal audit and the GRC Operational Task Libraries.

Exceptions require a documented risk assessment, compensating controls, a named owner, an expiry date (maximum 12 months) and approval per the delegated authority in the GRC Charter Section 6.1.

Violations may result in disciplinary action in accordance with local employment law, contract termination for third parties, and reporting to authorities where legally required. A click in a phishing simulation is not a violation.

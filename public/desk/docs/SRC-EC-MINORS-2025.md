2\. Key points

Point 1. On 14 July 2025 the European Commission published guidelines to help online platforms protect minors, under Article 28(4) of the Digital Services Act.

Point 2. The guidelines cover every online platform that is offered to people in the EU and can be used by minors (a service that stores its users' content and shows it to the public), wherever the provider is established, except micro and small enterprises (Article 19).

Point 3. They take a risk-based approach: each platform adopts the measures that fit the risks its own service poses to minors.

Point 4. Review the risks the service poses to minors, and act on the results.

Point 5. Set minors' accounts to the most protective settings by default, so that people they are not connected with cannot see or contact them.

Point 6. Check users' age with age assurance that is accurate, reliable and non-intrusive, without collecting more personal data than the check needs; self-declaration alone is not enough.

Point 7. Give minors' explicit choices priority over behavioural signals in AI-driven recommendations, and let them choose a feed that is not based on profiling.

Point 8. Let minors block and mute any user, and never add them to a group without their explicit consent.

Point 9. Switch off by default design features that drive excessive use, such as streaks, read receipts, autoplay and push alerts.

Point 10. The Commission will review the guidelines within 12 months.
